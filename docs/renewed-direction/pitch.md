# Part 1: The 7 Core Pillars (Quick-Reference Summary)

1. **The Ultimate Product Positioning & Metaphor**
* **Presenter vs. Real-Time Coach:** In the future, every student will have a world-class AI to prepare a "perfect slide deck (draft)," looking like a corporate executive on stage. But the moment a coach asks a real-time question about trade-offs, anyone who hasn't thought it through freezes. We are the rehearsal coach in the wings, forcing them to understand every layer of logic.
* **Judo Flip (Po-Shen Loh’s Insight):** Using AI to write your homework is like "driving a car for one mile to get exercise"—your brain gets zero workout. In the AI era, students must shift from learning *how to write homework* to learning *how to grade homework*.


2. **Unified Workflow: Git for Essay Thinking (Equal Treatment, Zero Surveillance)**
* **No Police, No Spyware:** Zero webcams, zero keystroke logging, and zero pseudoscientific "AI probability scores."
* **A Single Pipeline for Everyone:** Whether written from scratch (`0 -> 1`) or pasted from an external model (`1 -> 0`), all text enters the system as `Unstaged Changes`. To `Merge (Submit)`, everyone must complete the exact same **Span Binding** $\rightarrow$ **Commit Message** $\rightarrow$ **Coach PR Review**.


3. **Why 1-Shot LLM Prompts Inevitably Fail in Our System**
* **Finite Word-Budget Law:** Even with 2 trillion parameters, a 1,000-word essay is a "finite container." To fit inside 1,000 words, a 1-shot AI must secretly omit boundary conditions and mask data conflicts. Our system acts as a "Trade-Off Microscope."
* **Global Coherence Collapse:** When a student tries to patch Paragraph 2 with AI to answer a challenging data point (e.g., Gibbs Table 3 showing a 42% drop in junior mentoring), that patch shatters the premise of Paragraph 1 and the conclusion of Paragraph 3. Fixing the collapse forces a complete rewrite—by the time they finish four iterations, they have been forced to master the arguments and counter-evidence (*"Welcome to the AI era!"*).


4. **The 4 Cognitive Pillars (Upgrading the Paper with Every Question)**
Coach questions avoid childish fallacy quizzes, directly strengthening the student's stance and depth:
* **Reading:** Breaking AI’s "false completeness" by confronting the "Source Triads" and raw data tables (Table 3).
* **Logic:** Exposing unstated assumptions within strict word limits and throwing unseen counterfactual curveballs.
* **Wording (Defeating Token-Fog):** Catching diplomatic token-hedging (*"navigates the multifaceted interplay"*) and forcing students to use sharp, committed verbs.
* **Communication:** Integrating competing stakeholder perspectives (e.g., commuting junior vs. R&D head) to build multi-dimensional persuasive depth.


5. **Behavioral Nudge (Discouraging Whole-Paragraph Copy-Paste)**
* **Quick Inline Edits & Anchor Fragility:** The Coach locks onto just the specific 5–8 words that need fixing (taking 15 seconds to edit natively). If a student pastes a whole new AI paragraph from outside, it wipes out their linked source spans. Loss aversion keeps them thinking and editing inside the platform.


6. **The Ultimate Defense: Cross-Auditing & The 60-Second X-Ray Receipt**
* **Double-Standard Boomerang:** Even if an advanced user uses a "Super-Prompt" to fake excuses for discarded sources in the Graveyard (e.g., claiming a study of 1,200 people is "too small to generalize"), the system cross-audits it against their accepted sources (which use a study of 1,000 people), instantly catching their **Epistemic Double-Standard**.
* **60-Second Receipt + Oral Viva Bot:** Handing the professor a single-page evolution history with 3 targeted liability questions (to ask in office hours or trigger via the built-in voice bot).


7. **Institutional Compliance & Error Tolerance**
* **2026 WCAG 2.2 AA Accessibility:** Zero fine-motor drag-and-drop requirements, zero anxiety-inducing countdown timers.
* **Dispute / Override AI:** If a student disagrees with the Coach, they can click "Dispute" and write a 15-word defense, logging a "Gold-Star Critical Thinking Moment" on the teacher's receipt and turning AI imperfections into teaching opportunities.
