# Policy direction on assessment in the generative-AI era (as of 2026-10-14)

Scope: university and regulator policy direction, and what it implies for buying tools that verify understanding versus tools that detect AI. Each item is labelled **[POLICY TEXT]** (primary institutional or regulator document), **[REPORTING]** (news or secondary commentary) or **[SURVEY]**. Research date: 2026-10-14. Some primary pages (University of Melbourne, HKBU Young Reporter) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read; those claims rely on search snippets and are flagged.

## 1. University of Sydney "two-lane" assessment model

### Takeaway
Sydney splits assessment into **Lane 1 "secure"** (in-person, supervised, assessment *of* learning) and **Lane 2 "open"** (AI use allowed and scaffolded), and says most assessment should be in Lane 2. The framework took effect from Semester 2 2025. 2025 was spent mapping assessments, 2026 is for redesign, and by 2027 online programs will likely need in-person secure assessment. Sydney has published no figures on cost or scale for Lane 1. Its own guidance promotes interactive orals as cheaper in marking time than essays.

### Cited Findings
- **[POLICY TEXT]** Definition: "'Lane 1' assessments: These are designed to ensure students have mastered the skills and knowledge our programs require. They are supervised and designed for the 'assessment of learning'." Lane 2 (open) assessments "support the development of disciplinary knowledge, skills, and dispositions alongside AI." The university states that "most of our assessments should be in 'lane 2'." The new Sydney Assessment Framework started in Semester 2 2025. — [USyd Assessment & Feedback Framework (intranet)](https://intranet.sydney.edu.au/education-students/curriculum/assessment/assessment-feedback-framework.html); [Teaching@Sydney: Embracing the future of assessment](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/?p=19768)
- **[POLICY TEXT, institutional blog]** Lane 1 is "In-person, supervised tasks that confirm individual student achievement of the course learning outcomes", which may or may not involve AI. Timeline:
  - **2025:** map all assessments; faculties write per-degree Assessment Plans, agreed by the end of 2025.
  - **2026:** redesign curriculum and assessment at program level, aiming to show by the end of 2026 that every student pathway meets the Threshold Standards.
  - **Online programs:** a risk-based approval process applies for 2025–26. "By 2027" online programs are likely to need in-person assessment, at exam centres, partner institutions or residentials.

  Source: Adam Bridgeman and Danny Liu, published 2025-10-02 (page updated 2026-10-06). — [Teaching@Sydney: Navigating AI in Higher Education: tasks ahead for 2025 and 2026](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/navigating-ai-in-higher-education-tasks-ahead-for-2025-and-2026/)
- **[POLICY TEXT, institutional guidance]** Interactive orals (IOs) as a secure-lane option. Source: Kalman, Miller and Liu, 2024-04-18, drawing on Griffith's Logan-Fleming and Sotiriadou.
  - A 10–15 minute IO is estimated to assess about as much as a 3,000-word submission.
  - Suggested allowance is 20 minutes per student at first, dropping to 15 minutes.
  - Quotes: "the IO scales exceptionally well"; "educators who have given IO assessments say that they actually enjoy the marking"; "online assessments are not secure"; "Interactive oral assessments are fairly new to the University".
  - It warns that deepfake live video undermines online IOs.

  Source: [Teaching@Sydney: Five myths about interactive oral assessments](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/five-myths-about-interactive-oral-assessments-and-how-to-get-started/)
- **[REPORTING/opinion]** Critique of the two-lane approach: "threats to equity, limited validity of 'secure' tasks, poor pedagogy and erosion of academic freedom". — [Needed Now in L&T substack](https://needednowlt.substack.com/p/assessment-integrity-in-the-age-of)
- **[REPORTING]** The University of New England's version of the model says open-lane tasks cannot prohibit GenAI, though they may discourage it. — [UNE HASSE blog](https://blog.une.edu.au/hasse/?p=2644)

### Inferences
- The secure lane is a minority of tasks but is mandatory at program level, and it has to be *in-person/supervised*. A tool that runs an unsupervised remote viva would not count as Lane 1 under Sydney's definition. To count, it would need to run inside a supervised setting, or it sits in Lane 2 as formative evidence.
- Lane 2 is "most" assessment and allows AI. That creates demand for evidence of understanding *within* open tasks (process evidence plus viva-style checks), because the secure lane alone cannot cover every unit's outcomes.
- The mandatory move of online programs to in-person assessment by 2027 is a cost pressure that an "evidence on every submission" tool could ease. This is an assumption: Sydney has not said so.

### Gaps
- I found no published Sydney figures on the number of Lane 1 tasks, the number of orals run, staffing cost or workload. I could not confirm the share of Lane 1 that is exams versus orals versus interactive orals.

## 2. TEQSA (Australia) guidance and other Australian universities

### Takeaway
TEQSA has moved from education to regulation. Its sequence runs:
1. **2024:** a request for information (RFI) to all providers.
2. **May 2025:** announced it would be "regulatory-led from 2026".
3. **September 2025:** published *Enacting assessment reform in a time of artificial intelligence*, which offers three pathways and states that detection "is not viable".

Interactive orals are the named example of a unit-level secure task. Griffith runs them at cohort scale (up to 750 students). Practitioners report real workload problems at scale.

### Cited Findings
- **[POLICY TEXT]** TEQSA's AI RFI asked every registered provider for a "credible action plan", overseen by governance, on the risk GenAI poses to award integrity. All 203 providers responded to the July 2024 requirement. — [TEQSA: AI request for information, next steps](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/about-us/news-and-events/latest-news/artificial-intelligence-request-information-next-steps)
- **[POLICY TEXT]** *Enacting assessment reform in a time of artificial intelligence* (TEQSA, September 2025; co-authored by sector experts and facilitated by TEQSA's Higher Education Integrity Unit). — [TEQSA PDF](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/enacting-assessment-reform-in-a-time-of-artificial-intelligence.pdf)
- **[REPORTING on the policy text]** Jason Lodge, 2025-09-30, summarises the three pathways:
  1. **Program-wide reform:** multiple secure assessment points across a degree.
  2. **Unit-level assurance:** at least one secure task per unit, such as an "interactive oral task or in-class demonstration". It is quick to adopt, but "may fragment the student experience and increase administrative workload".
  3. **Hybrid:** program-wide principles for critical parts of a degree, unit-level assurance elsewhere.

  He reports that the resource says "detecting this use is not viable", warns against a "technological arms race of detection", and flags fully online and asynchronous assessment as vulnerable. — [FutureCampus](https://futurecampus.com.au/2025/09/30/from-compass-to-map-teqsa-charts-new-pathways-for-assessment-in-the-ai-era/); also [Instructure ANZ blog](https://www.instructure.com/en-au/resources/blog/three-pathways-assessment-reform-what-teqsas-ai-guidance-means-universities)
- **[REPORTING]** TEQSA announced in May 2025 that it would move to a regulatory-led approach from 2026. Providers must show how they manage AI risk under the Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards). — [Teaching@Sydney](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/?p=24402); [MinterEllison: TEQSA's 2026 regulatory changes](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/a-year-of-reform-regulatory-changes-and-developments-relating-to-teqsa-in-2026)
- **[REPORTING]** TEQSA's Corporate Plan 2026–30 (released 2026-08-31, per search summary) names generative AI among emerging risks to sector integrity. — [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/a-year-of-reform-regulatory-changes-and-developments-relating-to-teqsa-in-2026). I did not verify this against the plan itself.
- **[ACADEMIC]** A 2026 AJET national document analysis of 39 Australian institutions found that public documents mostly show "institutional positioning" rather than evidence of implemented assessment practice. — [AJET 11001](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/view/11001)
- **[ACADEMIC/REPORTING]** Griffith uses interactive oral assessments (IOAs) as an exam replacement for classes of up to 750 students (Logan et al. 2021). The interactive oral model comes from Sotiriadou et al. (2020). — [THE Campus: interactive orals and AI overreliance](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/interactive-orals-offer-solution-ai-overreliance-higher-education); [UNSW Teaching: oral assessment](https://www.teaching.unsw.edu.au/node/5780)
- **[ACADEMIC]** Droulers, Krautloher and Shaeri (2025, *Teaching in Higher Education*) surveyed 290 students across 25 subjects and found high engagement with IOAs. — [Griffith repository](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/26e4d681-ad59-4c3a-9ec9-cd59429508e6)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Practitioners who ran IOAs at scale report "significant workload issues to consider when conducting these types of assessments at scale". They advise checking booking, storage and admin systems first. — [HERDSA Connect: Interactive orals at scale](https://herdsa.org.au/herdsa-connect/interactive-orals-scale-what-happened-when-we-tried-draw-owl)
- **[EVENT]** The University of Melbourne CSHE symposium "Implementing Interactive Oral Assessments – What have we learned so far?" included a Monash panellist (A/Prof Erica Brady). — [Melbourne CSHE](https://melbourne-cshe.unimelb.edu.au/events/symposia-on-higher-education-research-informed-practice/interactive-oral-assessment); Macquarie guidance at [TECHE](https://teche.mq.edu.au/2023/10/oral-assessment/)

### Inferences
- Australian regulation now *requires* evidence of secure assurance of learning and explicitly rejects detection as a basis for it. A detection tool has no regulatory story in Australia. An understanding-verification tool that produces assurance evidence per unit fits the "unit-level assurance" pathway.
- The administrative workload of pathway 2 (one secure task per unit) is where tooling helps: scheduling, structured question generation from course content, recording, rubric marking and moderation of orals.

### Gaps
- I did not find TEQSA's own published outcomes of the 2026 regulatory approach, such as conditions imposed or findings on specific providers.
- The University of Melbourne policy page returned 403. A search snippet only indicates that it has a "University policy and actions" page on AI, so I could not confirm Melbourne's Turnitin AI-detector status. Monash and UNSW official IO policy pages were not retrieved.
- I found no hard cost per student for IOAs from a primary study.

## 3. UK: QAA, Russell Group, Jisc, use of vivas

### Takeaway
UK direction is "embed and adapt, don't ban". The Russell Group principles (July 2023) commit members to adapting assessment while protecting academic rigour. QAA is running assessment-focused sector activity. Vivas are emerging as *sampled* or *mini* vivas rather than universal ones. Student AI use is near-universal: HEPI 2026 found 94% use GenAI for assessed work.

### Cited Findings
- **[POLICY TEXT]** Russell Group principles on the use of generative AI tools in education (July 2023), endorsed by all 24 vice-chancellors. Five principles, including adapting teaching and assessment to incorporate ethical use while ensuring equal access, and upholding academic rigour and integrity. — [Russell Group principles](https://russellgroup.ac.uk/policy/policy-briefings/principles-use-generative-ai-tools-education); [announcement](https://russellgroup.ac.uk/news/new-principles-on-use-of-ai-in-education/)
- **[REPORTING]** QAA held its first Assessment & Feedback Roadshow in the last full week of March 2026, including AI sessions. — [QAA news](https://www.qaa.ac.uk/news-events/news/roadshow-draws-together-diverse-approaches-to-assessment)
- **[REPORTING]** At a Jisc National Centre for AI meetup, participants described institutional experiments with mini-vivas. Results were mixed: student anxiety and workload/scheduling problems on one side; on the other, some found vivas worked well as one part of a portfolio. — [Jisc NCAI blog](https://nationalcentreforai.jiscinvolve.org/wp/?p=7658)
- **[OPINION, THE]** Oxford and Sussex academics (March 2025) proposed that "a small, randomly selected percentage of students would complete a viva", run within existing moderation. — [THE: Sampled vivas are pivotal in combating AI cheating](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/735603)
- **[PRACTICE]** Newcastle University ran a capstone viva for a cohort of 60 with two markers present. The author notes the idea is "often quickly dismissed as expensive and unworkable". — [Newcastle blog](https://blogs.ncl.ac.uk/sin/?p=197)
- **[SURVEY]** HEPI and Kortext *Student Generative AI Survey 2026* (Report 199, March 2026; Savanta, December 2025, n=1,054 UK full-time undergraduates):
  - 95% use AI in some form.
  - 94% use GenAI to support assessed work.
  - Only 36% feel encouraged by their institution to use AI, and 38% are given AI tools.
  - The report notes "growing anxiety about misconduct accusations".

  Source: [HEPI report](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/); [PDF](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/HEPI-Report-199-Gen-AI-Survey-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Sampled vivas, which fit existing moderation, are the realistic UK buying entry point. A tool that lowers the per-viva cost and standardises questions directly addresses the "expensive and unworkable" objection.
- Student anxiety about false misconduct accusations supports a positioning built on positive evidence rather than detection.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 QAA or Jisc formal guidance document specific to vivas or orals, and no UK-wide data on the share of modules using orals.

## 4. Hong Kong (HKU, CUHK, HKUST, PolyU, CityU, HKBU, Lingnan; UGC)

### Takeaway
Hong Kong universities moved in 2023 from bans toward permitted, declared AI use, with instructors setting limits per assessment. HKU explicitly named oral exams and device-free exams as alternatives. Turnitin AI detection is licensed at least at HKUST. CUHK concedes false positives. HKBU's 2025 guidelines say detectors "may support, but not replace" judgment and allow instructors to request an "oral explanation of the work". I found no UGC-level assessment policy.

### Cited Findings
- **[POLICY TEXT]** HKU (2023) moved to fully integrate GenAI in teaching and learning. Its listed alternative assessments include "device-free examinations, oral examinations, live classwork such as demonstrations and presentations, and student peer assessments". — [HKU press release](https://hku.hk/press/news_detail_26434.html); [The Standard: HKU makes U-turn](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/54903/HKU-makes-U-turn-to-allow-ChatGPT-use). The Standard page shows a 2026 dateline but its content matches 2023.
- **[REPORTING]** HKU endorsed AI literacy as a "fifth literacy". — [FutureCIO](https://test.futurecio.tech/generative-ai-dubbed-as-fifth-literacy-at-university-of-hong-kong)
- **[POLICY TEXT]** HKUST has had Turnitin AI writing detection under its existing licence since Turnitin released it on 2023-04-05. — [HKUST CEI: Turnitin's AI Detection Capabilities](https://cei.hkust.edu.hk/en-hk/turnitin)
- **[POLICY TEXT]** HKBU *GenAI Advisory Guidelines (Summarised for students) 2025*:
  - Instructors may permit GenAI; students must declare use.
  - "AI detection tools may support, but not replace, professional academic judgment."
  - Instructors may request "AI usage records, early draft versions, oral explanation of the work".

  Source: [HKBU PDF](https://ar.hkbu.edu.hk/f/upload/6036/GenAI_Advisory_Guidelines__Summarised_version_for_students__2025.pdf)
- **[POLICY TEXT]** PolyU guidelines for students (2025-02-21): GenAI is allowed in take-home continuous assessment, with the student accountable. Teachers set limits in assignment briefs. AI-generated text, images and translations must be referenced. — [PolyU notice](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/ar/students-in-taught-programmes/notices-to-students/2025/2025-02-21_guidelines-for-students-on-the-use-of-generative-artificial-intelligence/); [PolyU EDC academic integrity](https://www.polyu.edu.hk/edc/explore-a-topic/academic-integrity/?sc_lang=en)
- **[POLICY TEXT]** CityUHK publishes GenAI guidelines with versions for up to Summer 2025 and from Semester A 2025/26. I did not read the content. — [CityU GenAI guidelines](https://www.cityu.edu.hk/GenAI/guidelines.htm)
- **[GOVERNMENT, 2023]** LegCo reply LCQ12 (2023-03-29) says Lingnan, CUHK, EdUHK, HKUST and HKU have issued GenAI guidelines for students, and that universities are considering adjusting assessment to strengthen critical thinking. — [info.gov.hk LCQ12](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202303/29/P2023032900296p.htm)
- **[REPORTING, student journalism, 2026-05-31]** The Young Reporter (HKBU) contacted 12 HK institutions:
  - CUHK "acknowledged the possibility of false positives" and said students can appeal via "fact-checking interviews".
  - A nursing student failed a course after a detector flag, and his appeal was rejected.

  Source: [The Young Reporter: AI detection tools spark false plagiarism fears](https://tyr-jour.hkbu.edu.hk/2026/05/31/ai-detection-tools-spark-false-plagiarism-fears-among-university-students/). The page returned 403; content is from the search snippet only.
- **[POLICY TEXT, sub-degree]** UOW College Hong Kong adopted a student GenAI policy in October 2025 covering assessments and exams. — [UOWCHK PDF](https://www.uowchk.edu.hk/site-assets/uow-college-hong-kong/college/studhome/studrules/Policy-on-Student-Use-of-Generative-AI-in-Learning-and-Assessment.pdf)

### Inferences
- Hong Kong already uses an oral follow-up as the *remedy* when detection is disputed: HKBU's "oral explanation" and CUHK's "fact-checking interviews". A structured, recorded, course-grounded viva tool slots into an existing process, and is more defensible than a detector score.
- Detection licences are already bundled (Turnitin), so the buyer is not paying separately for detection. The pitch is complementary, not a replacement.

### Gaps
- I found no UGC policy on GenAI assessment.
- No current policies were retrieved for Lingnan, CityU (content) or HKU in 2025–26.
- I could not confirm current VeriGuide use: it is CUHK-developed, but no 2025–26 source was found.
- I found no Hong Kong procurement-norm sources, such as tender thresholds or central versus faculty buying.

## 5. US/EU: blue books, oral exams, EU AI Act

### Takeaway
In the US, the shift is bottom-up. Blue books are back (Axios, March 2026) and oral exams are spreading (AP, April 2026: Cornell, Penn, NYU, UCSD). NYU Stern is already running an **AI-voiced oral exam** at home. In the EU, AI Act Annex III point 3 makes education AI used to evaluate learning outcomes **high-risk**. The Digital Omnibus postponed stand-alone Annex III obligations from 2026-08-02 to **2027-12-02**.

### Cited Findings
- **[REPORTING]** Axios (2026-03-14): blue books are making an "out of step" comeback. Critics say multilingual writers and students needing accommodations are "at a massive disadvantage". UC Davis professor Dan Melzer says educators won't "outsmart ChatGPT". — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/03/14/ai-blue-books-colleges-jobs)
- **[REPORTING]** AP, 2026-04-22, "Perfect homework, blank stares: Why colleges are turning to oral exams to combat AI":
  - **Cornell:** Chris Schaffer (biomedical engineering) runs 20-minute Socratic "oral defenses" on problem sets for 70 students. TAs now grade only the orals, not the written sets. Other Cornell courses use 30-minute "final conversations" and 4-minute mock interviews for a 180-student class. Cornell runs an Oral Assessment Workshop.
  - **Penn:** Bruce Lenthall describes a "massive shift toward in-person assessments".
  - **UC San Diego:** Huihui Qi ran a three-year study on scaling oral exams.
  - **NYU:** Clay Shirky reports more office hours, presentations and cold-calling. At NYU Stern, Panos Ipeirotis ran an AI-powered oral final using an ElevenLabs cloned professor voice. Students take it from home, it is AI-assisted in grading, and he plans to use it in all future classes. A student found it "choppy".

  Source: [WCAX/AP](https://www.wcax.com/2026/04/22/perfect-homework-blank-stares-why-colleges-are-turning-oral-exams-combat-ai/); also [WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/pennsylvania-university-colleges-oral-exams-artificial-intelligence/), [Chronicle: The return of the oral exam](https://www.chronicle.com/article/the-return-of-the-oral-exam)
- **[REPORTING]** YPulse (2026-04-28): students increasingly must orally defend written papers. — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2026/04/28/some-college-professors-are-reintroducing-oral-exams/)
- **[LAW, primary]** EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689) Annex III point 3 classifies the following as high-risk education AI: admission or assignment, **evaluating learning outcomes** (including when outcomes steer the learning process), assessing the appropriate level of education, and **monitoring and detecting prohibited behaviour of students during tests**. The text was not fetched this session; this is my knowledge of the adopted text. Cross-check: [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) lists "education" as an Annex III area.
- **[REPORTING, law firm]** Digital Omnibus on AI timeline (Gibson Dunn, written before formal adoption):
  - **2026-05-06:** provisional agreement.
  - **2026-05-13:** Council representatives confirm.
  - Stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations postponed to **2 December 2027**.
  - The Act's "risk-based approach, its governance structure, and its core obligations" are kept.

  Source: [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- **[REPORTING]** Adoption (secondary only):
  - **2026-06-16:** Parliament endorsed.
  - **2026-06-29:** Council final approval.
  - **2026-07-24:** published in the Official Journal as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 2026-07-27.
  - Annex I embedded products move to 2028-08-02. Article 50(2) watermarking grace period runs to 2026-12-02 for systems already on the market.

  Source: [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-high-risk-deadline-omnibus-20260/); [Secure Privacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-the-new-high-risk-ai-deadlines-after-council-approval); [Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/5190/digital-omnibus-ai-act-eu-parliament). I did not verify the regulation number or the Official Journal date against EUR-Lex.

### Inferences
- **EU, two exposures:**
  - A tool that *scores* understanding for grading falls under Annex III 3(b), evaluating learning outcomes. Provider obligations from 2027-12-02 include risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy and conformity assessment. Deployer obligations include human oversight and logging.
  - AI-cheating "detection" during tests falls under 3(d) and is also high-risk.

  A design where AI *recommends* an oral check and a human decides (the Acader rule-6 posture) reduces risk but likely does not remove the classification if the output feeds grading. That is my assumption; legal review is needed.
- The 2027-12-02 delay gives a window to build compliance in (logging, human-in-the-loop) as a selling point to EU buyers.
- The NYU Stern AI-voiced oral shows that faculty are already hand-building "AI viva" tools. The demand is real, but so is DIY competition.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the Annex III text from EUR-Lex or the Official Journal text of the Omnibus.
- I found no US federal or accreditor policy on AI and assessment.
- I found no data on blue-book sales volumes beyond anecdote.

## 6. Evidence of demand: faculty surveys, workload of orals, budgets

### Takeaway
Faculty concern about AI cheating is rising, from 36% in 2024 to 55% in 2026 (Tyton). 78% believe cheating has increased (AAC&U/Elon), and assessment redesign is the top practice change (47%). Orals cost roughly 15–20 minutes per student per round, plus scheduling overhead, which practitioners call the main barrier at scale. I found no budget data.

### Cited Findings
- **[SURVEY, secondary coverage]** Tyton Partners *Time for Class 2026* (instructors surveyed April–May 2026):
  - Faculty concern about cheating rose from 36% (2024) to 55% (2026).
  - 47% of faculty are modifying assessment design because of AI, and assessment is the number one practice being changed.

  Source: [D2L: five findings from Time for Class 2026](https://www.d2l.com/blog/five-findings-from-the-2026-time-for-class-report-that-surprised-us/); [Every Learner Everywhere](https://www.everylearnereverywhere.org/blog/time-for-class-2026-highlights-the-intersection-of-ai-integrated-assessment-and-student-engagement/). The primary report was not read.
- **[SURVEY]** AAC&U and Elon faculty survey (late 2025, published January 2026):
  - 78% say cheating has increased since GenAI.
  - "Increased a lot" is 58% per the chart; the Chronicle reports 57%.
  - 33% have had AI-related integrity cases.

  Source: [Elon/AAC&U charts PDF](https://imaginingthedigitalfuture.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ELON-AACU-AI-faculty-survey-charts-1_26.pdf); [Chronicle Teaching newsletter 2026-01-22](https://www.chronicle.com/newsletter/teaching/2026-01-22)
- **[SURVEY]** Chronicle survey on what counts as cheating: 52% of faculty say following a detailed AI-generated outline is cheating, and 29% say it is not. — [Chronicle](https://www.chronicle.com/newsletter/teaching/2026-01-22)
- **[SURVEY, 2024]** Wiley: 96% of instructors believe at least some students cheated. Planned responses include more in-person exams. — [eCampus News](https://www.ecampusnews.com/featured/2024/08/14/ai-hurt-academic-integrity-can-enhance-learning/)
- **[WORKLOAD]** Time per student and examples at scale:
  - Sydney guidance budgets 15–20 minutes per IO per student and claims this is less than marking a 3,000-word essay ([Teaching@Sydney](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/five-myths-about-interactive-oral-assessments-and-how-to-get-started/)).
  - Cornell's 70 students × 20 minutes is about 23 contact hours per round; 180 students × 4 minutes is about 12 hours ([AP/WCAX](https://www.wcax.com/2026/04/22/perfect-homework-blank-stares-why-colleges-are-turning-oral-exams-combat-ai/); my arithmetic).
  - HERDSA practitioners report "significant workload issues" at scale ([HERDSA](https://herdsa.org.au/herdsa-connect/interactive-orals-scale-what-happened-when-we-tried-draw-owl)).
  - Jisc meetup participants cite workload and scheduling problems ([Jisc NCAI](https://nationalcentreforai.jiscinvolve.org/wp/?p=7658)).
- **[REPORTING]** Detection reliability concerns: TEQSA says detection is "not viable" ([FutureCampus](https://futurecampus.com.au/2025/09/30/from-compass-to-map-teqsa-charts-new-pathways-for-assessment-in-the-ai-era/)). CUHK concedes false positives ([Young Reporter](https://tyr-jour.hkbu.edu.hk/2026/05/31/ai-detection-tools-spark-false-plagiarism-fears-among-university-students/)). HEPI reports student anxiety about misconduct accusations ([HEPI](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)).

### Inferences
- **Direction:** regulators (TEQSA) and policy (HKBU, Sydney) now treat detection as at most supporting evidence. They require secure or oral assurance of learning. Buying intent therefore shifts from "detect AI" to "produce defensible evidence of understanding at scale".
- **Pain point:** faculty time and scheduling for orals is the binding constraint that tools can relieve. That means asynchronous or structured orals, question generation grounded in course materials, recording and rubric-assisted marking with human sign-off.
- **Compliance:** in the EU, any tool that grades understanding is high-risk from 2027-12-02. Human-decides design and logging are required, not optional.
