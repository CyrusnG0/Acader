## Problem Statement
CS instructors can no longer trust take-home code. Similarity tools (MOSS, JPlag) miss AI-generated code and prose detectors do not transfer to code. A 763-educator ACM survey reports 68% have already moved toward oral exams, code-defense sessions, proctored exams and process-based assessment. Oral exams work but do not scale. The proposal's bet: automate the line-level viva, grounded in what the course actually taught, and hand the student a portable Proof of Understanding.

## Demand Evidence
None direct. Founder answer to Q1: "No direct evidence yet". The proposal rests on market reports, student-usage surveys and a validated technical prototype (lecture miner, 159 board states on MIT 18.06 L1). The one demand-side signal is third-party: the 68% assessment-shift survey and the SIGCSE 2026 AI-oral-exam talk. Neither shows anyone wants Acader.

## Status Quo
Unknown to the founder (Q2: "I don't know the real workaround yet"). Published evidence suggests: live code reviews, closed-book in-class quizzes weighted above take-home work, small-group conversational orals, trace/predict-output exercises. Real competitor = the free in-class quiz and the instructor's own oral spot-check.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
Target: a specific CS lecturer or FYP supervisor. Not yet named (Q3: "Category only, no named person"). Buyer/user split is unresolved: the person who feels the pain may not hold budget.
Narrowest wedge: an async "viva runner". The instructor pastes the assignment and the reference solution. The student submits code and answers 3-4 line-level questions. The instructor gets a one-page transcript report with no auto-verdict.

## Pilot Test Design (added by /plan-ceo-review, HOLD SCOPE, 2026-10-12)
Decisions D-011 to D-014 in docs/DECISIONS.md.
- **Blind comparison (premise 3):** For each student submission, generate two question sets: (1) graph stand-in = the instructor's lecture notes hand-structured into concepts, prerequisites and where each was taught; (2) the same lecture notes pasted raw. A generic no-notes control is optional. Sets are labeled X/Y in random order. The instructor rates each set per student; "cannot tell" counts as a tie. Decision rule: the graph stand-in must win at least 6 of 10, else premise 3 is dropped.
- **Generator guardrails:** student code is passed to the LLM as quoted data with fixed instructions. Every question must cite a real line number and quote its text, otherwise it is dropped and regenerated (max 2 retries). Empty, malformed, refused and timed-out outputs are handled distinctly, logged with the submission ID, and surfaced to the instructor as "generation failed" with a manual fallback.
- **Student data policy:** formative, ungraded practice viva. Plain-language consent note naming the LLM provider, what is stored and who sees it. Students may opt out and take the instructor's normal route. Instructor sees per-question signals; full transcripts only with the student's consent. Transcripts are deleted after 30 days and each student can export their own.

## Success Criteria
- 5 instructor interviews completed, each recorded in one paragraph of "what they did last semester".
- At least 1 named instructor agrees to run a real assignment through the pilot.
- Blind comparison on that assignment: graph-grounded questions rated better in at least 6 of 10 cases (or the graph premise is dropped).
- One instructor says unprompted that they would use it again or pay for it.
- Beats the free alternative (D-020): in the optional side-by-side with NYU Viva, at least 3 of the first 5 instructors would choose ours, or the reason they would not is recorded and acted on.
