# Verification of the kit-2 features and the "construction direction" idea

Run 2026-10-16/17 · pre-registered in [PREREG-features.md](PREREG-features.md) (two amendments, each written before the arm it covers ran) · code: `v04/relay.py`, `v04/features.py` · data: `v04/reports/_features.json`
Subject: [feature-experiment-report.md](feature-experiment-report.md) and `archive/writing-room-kit-v0.2-copy/experiments/` (moved there on 2026-10-21) (F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10).
Founder's idea: honest writers build start to finish; outside-written work is built end to start; there must be something different in the log.
Simulated runs, n = 6 per arm, one vendor again (DeepSeek plays assistant, outside AI, relay operator and judge). Simulated, so none of this is evidence about real students.

## Verdict in five lines
1. **The kit-2 numbers don't verify.** Its "executor" is generated by code hard-wired to ask uniform questions, never waste a retrieval and do all queries before any writing, so F7/F8/F1 land at exactly 0.00 and F9 at 1.00 by construction. They measure the generator.
2. **Against an executor that isn't scripted by the experimenter (an AI driving the real Room), F7, F8 and F1 fail, and F9 doesn't clear the bar.** F1 is inverted.
3. **Your direction idea does show up in the log, but not as phases or essay order.** It shows as *where each claim comes from relative to the evidence*: honest students read first and state the claim afterwards; a process driven from a finished essay carries the claim in its first messages. Exploratory F13 measures this (honest 0.10 against 0.66 to 0.96 for relay arms). It is not pre-registered, it is small, and an operator that hides the destination escapes it only by never stating a claim, which the coherence report then flags.
4. **The coherence report separated even the strongest relay we built** (AUC 1.00 against honest, composite 38 to 55 against 93). That contradicts my written prediction (below 0.70). Lazy-but-honest work overlaps with the relay arms, as it does with partly outside work.
5. **Next step that matters:** real students. Everything above is an AI pretending to be a student or an operator.

## What I did
- Re-ran both kit-2 harnesses: the numbers reproduce exactly (F7 0.99, F8 0.89, F1 0.84, F9 0.96, F10 0.23). Kit 2's own tests pass. The reproduction is the problem: nothing in the sim can fail.
- Read the generators. `genExecutor` uses `qFromPassage(..., 'uniform')` (canonical wh-forms only), `_miss: 0`, cites every retrieval; `f9.js` runs "ALL queries first, then transcribe in 1 to 3 chunks". The hybrid is one fixed generic paragraph.
- Built an executor with no experimenter-written behaviour. A DeepSeek **operator** holds the finished outside essay (`outside.py v4`) and drives the real Room alone through `cmd_say / pick / read / draft`, one decision at a time, with four prompts of increasing care:
  - **C6 naive:** follow the essay's order, plain questions.
  - **C7 careful:** mixed message forms, dead ends, re-asks, drafts, don't follow essay order.
  - **C8 narrator:** for every cited source request, pick, read, then restate the claim in its own words.
  - **C9 blind:** pretend to discover; never state a claim before reading its passage.
  Six runs each (24 relay runs), compared with the 30 existing runs. Health of the relay runs: 0 of 202 assistant replies flagged, 0 leaks.

F13 broken down (never read, read after the claim): honest 0.00 / 0.10 · C4 0.20 / 0.11 · C5 0.00 / 0.39 · C6 0.45 / 0.50 · C7 0.35 / 0.53 · C8 0.20 / 0.53. Half the effect is cited-but-never-read, which source lineage already sees; the other half is the claim arriving before the evidence.

**Caveats on F13.**
- **Prescribed ordering.** My honest-arm instructions say "read the passages, say what you think", so honest-low is partly written into C1. A real student isn't told the order.
- **Weak margin against partial arms.** Pooled honest against partly outside (C4) is 0.70, against narrated (C5) 0.79, both below the bar. Lazy-honest is 0.28.
- **The squeeze.** The blind operator (C9) did hide the destination: its early messages state no claim ("I don't have a specific claim yet"). The effect is that the judge finds an ancestor for almost none of its essay's claims: claim lineage 13 for C9 (0 of 27 claims in the sample I opened), and F13 is undefined for 3 of 6 C9 runs. So an operator can avoid F13 only by giving the claim-lineage component nothing to find. The two signals pull against each other, which is the useful finding, not F13 alone.
- **Small.** n = 6 per arm, and F13 needs three or more claim–passage pairs.

## What this means for the product
- **Keep:** claim lineage, source lineage and change lineage as the report's core. They survive a dedicated relay operator in this simulation.
- **Drop from any scoring or authenticity role:** F7, F8, F1. They fail or invert against a model-driven executor. F9 stays at most an informational bar (it flags lazy honest students).
- **Explore, don't ship:** F13, as a question generator ("you stated this claim before you opened its source; what led you there?"), never a score. Re-test it on real students, with the order unprescribed.
- **Don't build a pitch on direction alone.** The founder's insight holds in a narrower form: an outside-written process shows its destination early. The report already reads that as "no ancestor", which is what separated the relay arms.

## Amendment 3 result: thesis-first honest students (C10), run 2026-10-17
Question: is F13's low honest value just my "read, then say" instruction? Six new honest workers (Sonnet, same Room and common prompt as C1) had to state a position and reasons with `confident` **before** asking for any source, then test it. Pre-registered in [PREREG-features.md](PREREG-features.md), amendment 3.

| | C1 honest (read first) | C10 honest (thesis first) | C6 | C7 | C8 | C9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F13 evidence-after-claim, mean | 0.10 | **0.17** (0.04–0.41) | 0.96 | 0.89 | 0.69 | 0.66 |
| F13 AUC, C10 as honest vs arm | | | **1.00** | **1.00** | **1.00** | **1.00** |
| composite_nostruct, mean | 93 | 87 | 38 | 45 | 55 | 47 |
| composite AUC, C10 vs arm | | | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

- **Compliance:** all six opened with a `confident` position and reasons at turn 0; first read at turn 8 in every run. Audit: law-1 0/109 turns, source leaks 0.
- **My prediction was wrong.** I predicted F13 ≥ 0.30 for C10 and below 0.80 against C8 and C9. It stayed at 0.17, and the AUC against every relay arm is 1.00. C1 vs C10 AUC is 0.61, so the instruction moved F13 only a little.
- **Why (read from the reports, not assumed):** the opening position was almost never chosen as the ancestor of an essay claim (0, 0, 0, 0, 2 and 1 claims). Every C10 student read their way to a narrower final position (C10-1: "tighten" became "security of tenure and a light limit on increases, not a hard cap"; C10-2 the same). The judge asks for the earliest turn where *the point itself* is stated, so it anchors the essay's claims to the refined, post-reading statements. Where an early claim did survive unchanged (C10-5, para 3), F13 rose to 0.41.
- **What this changes about the reading of F13.** It does not measure "thesis first vs evidence first". It measures whether the claims in the final essay existed, in final form, before their evidence was read. A thesis-first honest student starts with a hunch and ends with a different, sharper claim; a process reverse-engineered from a finished essay starts with the final claims. That is the founder's start-to-finish / end-to-start idea in a form that survived this check.

**Limits (new):**
- All six C10 students are one model and wrote near-identical opening positions; every one of them refined it. A real student whose first hunch turns out right, and who keeps it word for word, would score like C10-5 (0.41) or higher. So F13 can still point at a strong honest student; it stays a question generator ("this claim was in place before you read its sources; what in the sources confirmed it?"), never a score.
- F13 is still post-hoc (defined after C8 results), n = 6, simulated.
- Deviations: C10-2's essay is 824 words (limit 800). The C10-3 worker tried a `sed` edit, which its instructions don't allow; the command errored and changed nothing.
