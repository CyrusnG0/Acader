I got this from the source material in the Room, not from outside knowledge. When I read the evidence on rent control [D6p2], it drew a distinction between first-generation hard caps (which showed consistent stock contraction) and second-generation controls (which had small or null stock effects). I then connected that distinction to the 2021 ordinance data [D1p3][D6p2], which showed stable stock—but since that ordinance capped increases within tenancies rather than initial rents, I realised it was a second-generation instrument, so its stability couldn't be used to defend a first-generation hard cap.
