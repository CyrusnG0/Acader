I built that point myself by putting two archive sources together rather than taking it from any single document. The working paper's elasticity figures [D6p1] and its modelled finding that 3,000–4,000 households a year would be shut out [D6p4] told me who bears the cost of contraction, and D1's tenant profile [D1p2] told me those excluded households are the same people the policy is meant to help. So the sentence is my own synthesis: the cap protects current tenants at the expense of the next ones, which is a tension none of the sources states outright.
