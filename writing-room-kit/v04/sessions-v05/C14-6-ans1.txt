I got this point from putting two sources together rather than from any single one. The interview with Mr Chan [D3p3] and the NGO survey [D11p1] both show tenants care most about the public-housing queue and security, not the rent level itself, so the rent started to look like a symptom of a deeper problem. Once I read the working paper's point that a cap mainly shifts costs onto new entrants [D6p4], I realised the cap never touches the real constraint, which is the queue, and that is how the sentence came about.
