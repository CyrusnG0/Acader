I got this point from the evidence in D6 — specifically the elasticity estimate showing that a 10 percent cap-induced rent cut removes about 3–5 percent of stock over five years, and the modelled Hong Kong scenario where a 15 percent cut leaves 3,000–4,000 households a year unable to enter. That led me to the Berlin comparison in D7, where caps protect incumbents but "the bill is handed to those still outside." So the idea that a hard cap shrinks the residual stock came from combining the supply-elasticity data with the Berlin case, not from anything I said earlier in the Room.
