I got this point from the working paper's model in [D6p4], which shows that a 15 percent cut in initial rents would block 3,000 to 4,000 households a year from entering the segment, and those households are mostly new arrivals and newly formed ones. I connected that to the primer's point in [D1p2] that these groups are the segment's core tenants, so the people shut out are exactly the ones with the fewest alternatives. That's how I ended up framing it as the households most in need bearing the cost of contraction.
