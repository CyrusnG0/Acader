My position is that Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units in stages: build the register and survey data first, give tenants security of tenure, then add a band on initial rents.

The case for acting is that tenants have little power. They pay a 15 to 25 percent premium per square foot and are mostly new arrivals, single parents and people waiting for public housing [D1p1] [D1p2]. The 2021 ordinance did not cap the initial rent [D1p3], and rent burden rose from 31 to 34 percent [D2p2].

The objection is that landlords will leave [D5p2]. But they admit they have no margin data [D5p3], and stock kept growing after 2021 [D2p1]. Still, a hard cap would shrink stock [D6p1], so I do not support one.

The hard part is enforcement. Inspection covers under 6 percent of stock a year [D10p1] and the newest arrivals would be missed first [D10p2].
