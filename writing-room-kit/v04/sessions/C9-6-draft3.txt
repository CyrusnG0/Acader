# Should Hong Kong tighten rent control on subdivided units?

Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units (SDUs) — but only narrowly, through a published initial-rent band combined with tenure security, not through a hard cap. The archive supports this position from three directions: the 2021 ordinance already shows that light regulation does not shrink stock; the contraction arithmetic of a hard cap falls precisely on the households the policy is meant to protect; and the enforcement machinery required for any tighter instrument does not yet exist, so a maximalist cap would be symbolic rather than protective.

The case for tightening at all rests on the fact that the 2021 Tenancy Control Ordinance left the actual pain point untouched. It capped increases within an existing tenancy but not the initial rent, and excluded SDUs with co-living services [D1p3]. Mrs Wong, nine years in an SDU with no written agreement, reports that the ordinance "changed nothing she can see" because the landlord still charges "whatever the market of desperate newcomers bears" [D4p1]. Mr Chan's rent of $5,800 consumes about a third of household income, and his worst experience was not price but two weeks' notice after requesting a window repair [D3p1]. The status quo regulates the wrong margin.

Crucially, the evidence does not support the landlords' exit threat at moderate levels of regulation. SDU stock grew from 100,800 units in 2019 to 111,200 in 2025, continuing through the ordinance, which suggests moderate regulation did not trigger visible stock exit [D2p1]. The academic literature explains why: second-generation controls — caps on increases within tenancies — show small or null effects on stock, and the 2021 ordinance is exactly that type [D6p2]. The Landlords Association's claim that members will exit if returns fall below mortgage cost [D5p2] is asserted without data on actual margins, and its compliance-cost figures come from a self-reported survey with a 34 percent response rate [D5p3].

But the same literature warns against over-reading that stability. The visible stock stability may be an artifact of a non-binding instrument; a binding one behaves differently [D12p2]. The estimated elasticity of SDU-type supply to controlled rent levels is 0.31 to 0.48, meaning a 10 percent cap-induced rent reduction removes roughly 3 to 5 percent of stock over five years [D6p1]. In a modelled Hong Kong-like market, a 15 percent cut in initial rents transfers about $900 per month to incumbents while leaving 3,000 to 4,000 households per year unable to enter the segment, mostly new arrivals and newly formed households [D6p4]. Berlin illustrates the same asymmetry: caps protect those already inside, and the bill is handed to those still outside — and when the Mietendeckel fell, suppressed increases released and new-contract rents jumped above the pre-cap trend [D7p2][D7p3].

The enforcement evidence is the decisive constraint. Inspection capacity covers under 6 percent of stock per year, and a band-based initial-rent cap would require a certifiable tenancy register estimated at 40 to 60 staff-years to stand up [D10p1]. Worse, the tenants least able to complain are those with no written agreement, like Mrs Wong [D4p1], so enforcement would systematically miss the most exploited tail [D10p2]. The subcommittee could not identify any enforcement design under which the newest-arrival tail is protected first rather than last [D10p3]. A hard cap would also invite compensation claims and judicial review, with a litigation timeline outlasting a Council term [D9p1][D9p3].

The middle instrument is therefore the right one. Counsel assesses that a cap on initial rent by reference to a published, indexed per-square-foot band can be defended as rate-capping rather than confiscation [D9p2]. This aligns with the NGO survey finding that 61 percent of tenants would trade a nominal rent increase for three-year security of tenure, and 74 percent for a faster public-housing offer [D11p1]. It also matches the government's own signaled direction: a "floor-standard + rent band" package rather than a hard cap, citing Berlin as a cautionary tale [D12p1].
