Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but not by capping starting rents. It should tighten limits on rent increases, give tenants three years of security of tenure, and spend enforcement effort on conditions.

Tenants are in a weak position. About 110,000 households live in these flats and pay 15 to 25 percent more per square foot than people in ordinary flats [D1p1]. Many are new arrivals waiting for public housing [D1p2].

The 2021 law capped increases only [D1p3], and stock kept growing [D2p1]. A hard cap is different: a model of a 15 percent cut in starting rents finds incumbents gain but 3,000 to 4,000 households a year cannot enter [D6p4].

Objection one: enforcement will miss the worst cases. Inspection covers under 6 percent of stock [D10p1] and the most exploited tenants complain least [D10p2]. This narrows my claim: rent rules alone are not enough.

Objection two: landlords will leave. Their association admits it has no margin data [D5p3], and stock grew under the 2021 law [D2p1].
