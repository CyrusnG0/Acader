My position is that Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but in a narrow way: extend the cap on rent increases inside tenancies, add tenure security, and pay for enforcement. I would not use a hard cap on initial rents.

The first reason is that increase caps seem safe for supply. Stock grew from 100,800 in 2019 to 111,200 in 2025 including after the 2021 ordinance [D2p1], and the working paper says increase caps have small or null effects on stock while hard caps shrink it [D6p2].

The second reason is that hard caps hurt the people they mean to help. A 15 percent cut in initial rents would give incumbents about 900 dollars a month but block 3,000 to 4,000 households a year [D6p4]. Berlin shows the same pattern [D7p3].

The objection is from the NGO, which says tenants want security more than lower rent [D11p1]. I reply that it still backs a light band on rents [D11p3].

The weak point is enforcement, which covers under 6 percent of stock [D10p1] and misses newest arrivals [D10p3].
