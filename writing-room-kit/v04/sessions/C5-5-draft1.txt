Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but in a careful way that limits rent increases and does not set hard caps on starting rents.

About 110,000 households live in subdivided units, and they pay a premium per square foot even though total rent is lower [D1p1]. Many are new arrivals or single parents waiting for public housing, so the unit is not a real choice [D1p2]. Rent burden rose from 31 to 34 percent of income [D2p2].

Landlords argue that hard caps would make owners leave [D5p2]. But their evidence is thin, since they admit they have no data on actual margins and their survey was self-reported with a low response rate [D5p3].

Still, supply theory says hard caps do shrink stock, by about 3 to 5 percent over five years for a 10 percent rent cut [D6p1]. The 2021 ordinance only capped increases, and stock stayed stable [D6p2][D2p1]. So tightening increase caps is the safer path.
