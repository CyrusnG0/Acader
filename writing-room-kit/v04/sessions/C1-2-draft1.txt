I think Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units only in a limited way, and should not bring in a hard cap on initial rents.

About 110,000 households live in subdivided units, and they pay 15 to 25 percent more per square foot than people in ordinary flats [D1p1]. Many have no choice because the public housing wait is about five and a half years [D1p2]. The 2021 ordinance only capped increases inside a tenancy [D1p3], and the stock of units kept growing afterwards [D2p1].

Berlin shows the risk of a hard cap. Listings fell within a year because owners held back flats [D7p1], and the people protected were those already inside [D7p3]. The academic paper says hard caps on levels reduce supply, while caps on increases do not [D6p2].
