I think Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but not by capping rent levels. The tightening should protect tenants from being pushed out and limit rises inside a tenancy.

A hard cap is risky. A working paper finds that supply of this kind of housing falls when controlled rents fall, by about 3 to 5 percent of stock for a 10 percent cut [D6p1]. The people who lose units are the same people the cap is meant to help [D6p3]. Landlords say they would leave the market [D5p2], although their data is weak and self-reported [D5p3].

The current rule only limits rises inside a tenancy, and stock kept growing after it [D2p1, D6p2]. But rents still rose faster than general rents and rent burden got worse [D2p2]. So the current rule is not enough.

Tenants care about security. Mr Chan says insecurity is worse than price [D3p1], and a survey found 61 percent would accept a rent increase for three years of security [D11p1].
