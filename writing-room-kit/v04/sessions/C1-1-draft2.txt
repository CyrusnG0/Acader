Hong Kong should tighten its rules on subdivided units, but narrowly. The extra rules should give tenants more security of tenure and add a light limit on initial rents. They should not be a hard cap.

The current rule has not fully protected tenants. Median rent per square foot rose 3.1 percent under the 2021 ordinance, against 1.7 percent for general private rents, and rent burden rose from 31 to 34 percent [D2p2]. So leaving things alone is not enough.

Hard caps are a different matter. A working paper estimates that a 10 percent cap-induced rent cut removes roughly 3 to 5 percent of stock over five years [D6p1]. Berlin shows what this looks like: supply of listings fell within a year [D7p1], and the tenants protected were those already inside, while those outside paid the bill [D7p3].

The landlords say they would leave if returns fell below mortgage cost [D5p2]. But they admit they have no data on actual margins [D5p3].
