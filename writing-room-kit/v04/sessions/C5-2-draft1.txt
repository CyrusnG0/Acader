Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but only in a narrow way: stronger limits on increases inside tenancies, tenure security, and better enforcement, not a hard cap on initial rents.

Tenants in subdivided units are badly served. They pay a 15 to 25 percent premium per square foot compared with ordinary flats [D1p1], and the rent burden for these households rose from 31 to 34 percent of income [D2p2]. Many of them are new arrivals or families waiting for public housing, so the unit is not a real choice [D1p2].

The usual fear is that landlords will leave. A hard cap on rent levels does seem to shrink supply, with a 10 percent cap-induced cut removing roughly 3 to 5 percent of stock [D6p1]. But caps on increases within a tenancy show small or null effects, and the 2021 ordinance is of this kind [D6p2]. The stock kept growing after it [D2p1]. The landlords' threat to exit is weak evidence because they admit they have no margin data [D5p3].

Tenants themselves care more about security than price: 61 percent would accept a small rent rise for three years of security [D11p1]. Enforcement is also a limit, since inspections cover under 6 percent of stock a year [D10p1]. So tightening should be modest and paired with tenure security.
