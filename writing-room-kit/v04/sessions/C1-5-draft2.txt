Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but only narrowly: stronger security of tenure and a light band on initial rents, not a hard cap.

Subdivided units are cubicles carved out of flats, home to about 110,000 households, who pay a premium per square foot [D1p1]. Their rent burden rose from 31 to 34 percent of income [D2p2], so doing nothing is not fine. Mr Chan says insecurity is the worst part, since a landlord ended his tenancy after he asked for a repair [D3p1]. In a survey of 440 households, 61 percent would take three-year tenure security over avoiding a rent increase [D11p1].

The objection is that landlords will leave. The working paper says hard caps on rent levels cut supply, and a 15 percent cut in initial rents helps current tenants by about 900 dollars a month while 3,000 to 4,000 households a year cannot enter [D6p2, D6p4]. Mr Chan saw two units emptied [D3p2], and Mrs Wong saw two flats become storage [D4p3].

My reply is that caps on increases within a tenancy did not shrink the stock, which grew from 100,800 to 111,200 units [D2p1, D6p2]. So tightening the same kind of rule is low risk.
