Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units, but in a careful way. Tighten limits on rent increases and on evictions, and do not impose a hard cap on starting rents.

About 110,000 households live in subdivided units, and their tenants pay 15 to 25 percent more per square foot than people in normal flats [D1p1]. Rents in these units rose faster than general private rents, and the share of income spent on rent went from 31 to 34 percent [D2p2]. These are people who have few choices, such as new arrivals and families waiting for public housing [D1p2].

Tenant Mr Chan says the worst part is not price but insecurity. His landlord ended his tenancy with two weeks notice after he asked for a repair [D3p1]. So rules on eviction matter as much as rent levels.

The risk is that landlords leave the market. The landlords association says owners will exit if returns fall below mortgage cost [D5p2]. Research suggests hard caps do reduce supply, but controls on increases within tenancies show small effects on stock [D6p2]. Hong Kong stock kept growing after the 2021 ordinance [D2p1].

So I support tightening, but limited to rises and eviction protection.
