Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units only narrowly: keep and strengthen the cap on increases, add tenure security and perhaps a light band on starting rents, but not a hard cap on initial rent [D11p3].

The problem is real. About 110,000 households live in subdivided units, and per square foot they pay 15 to 25 percent more than tenants in mainstream flats [D1p1]. Many are new arrivals, single parents and people waiting for public housing, which takes about five and a half years [D1p2]. Rent burden rose from 31 to 34 percent of income [D2p2].

The 2021 ordinance capped increases but not the initial rent [D1p3]. Stock kept growing after it [D2p1]. A working paper explains why: hard caps on levels cause consistent contraction, while caps on increases have small or null effects [D6p2].

A hard cap is risky. The paper estimates a 10 percent cap-induced rent cut removes about 3 to 5 percent of stock over five years [D6p1], and the households that lose units are the same ones the cap should protect [D6p3]. Berlin shows this: new listings fell after its 2020 cap [D7p1], and the tenants protected were those already inside while the bill went to those outside [D7p3].
