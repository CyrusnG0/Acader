Hong Kong should tighten its rules on subdivided units, but the tightening should be about security of tenure and a light band on initial rents, not a hard cap on rent levels.

My first idea was just to cap rents harder. The evidence made me change it. The 2021 ordinance capped increases inside a tenancy, and the stock still grew from 100,800 to 111,200 units [D2p1]. A working paper explains that this kind of control has small effects on supply, while hard caps on levels consistently shrink it [D6p2]. When units disappear, the poor lose them [D6p3]. Mr Chan has seen two units on his floor emptied [D3p2].

But the current rule is not enough. Rent burden rose from 31 to 34 percent [D2p2], and the 2021 law did not cap initial rent [D1p3]. Most SDU tenants are new arrivals or people waiting for public housing [D1p2]. In a survey of 440 households, 61 percent would trade a small rent rise for three years of security [D11p1].

Enforcement is the problem. Inspections cover under 6 percent of stock [D10p1] and the people least able to complain are those without written agreements [D10p2]. Mrs Wong has never had one and says the ordinance changed nothing for her [D4p1]. Mr Chan lost a tenancy after asking for a repair [D3p1].
