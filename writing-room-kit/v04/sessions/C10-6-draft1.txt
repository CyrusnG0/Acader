I first thought Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units because tenants are poor and have little power. After reading the archive I still say tighten, but not with a hard cap on starting rents.

About 110,000 households live in subdivided units, and they pay 15 to 25 percent more per square foot than people in normal flats [D1p1]. Many are new arrivals or single parents waiting for public housing [D1p2]. The 2021 ordinance only capped rises inside a tenancy [D1p3], and burden still went from 31 to 34 percent of income [D2p2].

The objection is that a hard cap cuts supply. A working paper models a 15 percent cut in initial rents and finds a gain for people inside but thousands per year locked out [D6p4]. Berlin shows the same pattern [D7p3].
