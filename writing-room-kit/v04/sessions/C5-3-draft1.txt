Hong Kong should tighten rent control on subdivided units only narrowly. It should add security of tenure and perhaps a light band on initial rents, but it should not bring in a hard cap.

Subdivided units matter because many people have no other choice. About 110,000 households live in them, and tenants pay a premium per square foot [D1p1]. They are often new arrivals, single parents and people waiting for public housing, so the unit is a residual option [D1p2].

A hard cap has a real cost. The working paper estimates that a 10 percent cap-induced rent cut removes roughly 3 to 5 percent of stock over five years [D6p1]. The households who lose units are the ones the cap was meant to protect [D6p3].

Tenants also say rent is not their main problem. In a survey of 440 households, 61 percent would trade a rent increase for three years of tenure security [D11p1]. So security of tenure is the better tightening.

The landlords say they will leave if returns fall [D5p2], but their data is weak and self-reported [D5p3]. I still think the supply risk from a hard cap is serious because it fits the working paper.
